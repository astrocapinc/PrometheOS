# Product and requirements

PrometheOS connects the scientific and human record around agent-assisted research. The everyday setup is a preferred coding agent plus a calm companion view. The agent implements, simulates and writes. PrometheOS supplies context, records meaningful outcomes and corrections, reconciles meetings with current work, exposes evidence and documents, and helps decide the next useful step.

Positioning (A05, D14): PrometheOS is the researcher-owned ledger beneath whatever AI the researcher uses. It owns the records (sources and versions, claims and evidence, receipts, corrections, decisions, work items, deadlines and events) and leaves searching, reading, analysing, drafting and rendering to the agents and tools the researcher chooses (D13). Under A07 (D16) it also brings a small, guarded AI of its own for typed proposals (classify, link, outline, word a card), which code verifies and the researcher can turn off. It is a product for other researchers as well as its first user: it ships with an installer, documentation, generic adoption of existing folders and support for any MCP-capable agent.

Initial user: a simulation researcher working with supervisors and coauthors on several related papers. Initial environment: Windows, local project files, MATLAB/Simulink executed by the user's existing tools. The hub must be useful to one person even if collaborators never install it.

Operating objective (A02), not a claim that any of it exists yet: install PrometheOS, connect a coding agent, optionally configure one model provider or a local model, and work. Further accounts are needed only for integrations the researcher chooses. Every feature is reachable by the coding agent through MCP and the local HTTP API, and the app is equally complete for a researcher who never types an agent prompt or a terminal command.

## Experience

Resume: the agent retrieves current objective, scoped findings, recent corrections, meeting commitments, waiting items and source coverage. It fetches detail only when needed. Researcher selects work, agent executes, records an attributed result and artifact references, and the notebook explains what changed. Under A02 a closed chat loses nothing: running operations, external runs that finished but await interpretation, pending approvals and dependents flagged by a correction are all in the record, so neither the agent nor the researcher rebuilds a long prose handoff.

Literature (A02): the researcher imports a bibliography and PDFs or reads their local Zotero library, searches it locally and builds an evidence matrix in which every supported cell points to a page and quotation, with abstract-only and missing full text shown as such. Later checkpoints add saved questions with opt-in public-API discovery, screening, cited synthesis and quiet watches. A model's memory is never a source, and a missing search result never proves novelty.

Meeting: a recollection, transcript, uploaded recording or supported live recording enters the project. Original material, transcription, interpretation and adopted actions remain distinct. The account identifies requests already addressed, changes in conditions, uncertain owners/dates and conflicting directions. At the next meeting an agenda connects commitments to delivered evidence.

Paper: a selected manuscript statement leads to its claim, evidence conditions, figure and original source. Changed evidence marks the relevant section for review. A local PDF is labelled with its actual source version. LaTeX manuscripts can be edited, compiled and synchronised with Overleaf in the hub workspace (A01); other native authoring remains external. Multiple papers can share evidence while recording distinct contributions and potential overlap.

Skills: install a known version of a scientific procedure, inspect its inputs/tools/licenses, adapt with provenance, run it through the agent, and save the research output. Community packages do not acquire PrometheOS or host permissions simply by declaring them.

## Scope and release profiles

**Developer prototype (CP0–CP2):** real local core/CLI, synthetic and personal non-sensitive sample data. Not yet advertised as a usable full hub.

**Pilot (CP0–CP6 technical, live host + researcher evidence):** core, meetings, paper links, skills, connected hub and optional audio/synthesis. May truthfully publish an import-only recording limitation while direct capture is unresolved; that does not satisfy R12.

**Full intended v1:** all R01–R27 and R35–R37 accepted: R01–R19, the CP10 and CP11 requirements R20–R27 added by A02, and the CP17–CP19 requirements R35–R37 added by A05 (adoption, work items and the queue, the desktop application and agent package). That includes real Windows online audio capture, academic workflows, recurring work, complete agent/app parity, research continuity and the local literature library. The clean-release gate (CP8) is accepted on a candidate that contains CP10, CP11 and CP17–CP19. Individual optional cloud connectors may remain unsupported and must be omitted from claims. A field result is required for R17.

**Self-contained research profile (later, A02):** Full intended v1 plus R28–R34 (CP12–CP16): CPU-first local AI, literature discovery and watches, deliverable recipes and selected connectors, so that ordinary research work needs no research-SaaS subscription and no account beyond the integrations the researcher chooses. It adds to v1 and waives nothing in it. E07 and the CP9 Overleaf scope stay required. A release claim for this profile needs its own release-candidate regression and field evidence, defined by a later amendment. Until then the capability matrix reports CP12–CP16 capabilities individually. Under A07, CP21 (the web-first application) and CP22 (the intelligence layer) are in no release profile yet and their capabilities are reported individually, like CP20's.

**Optional backlog (A02, required by no profile):** adopted only on demonstrated need through a later amendment. It includes a hosted decision backend such as TypeSafe's Jev, PaperQA2 and GROBID adapters, a larger local generation tier, mail, cloud storage, Microsoft Graph, task, note and GitHub connectors, specialist adapters (notebooks, pipelines, experiment tracking, rendering, deposits, lab systems, teaching, grants, ORCID, automation engines), paid research accelerators, cross-project portfolio views and forks of upstream components. No storage is built on OSF Projects writes.

**Future, excluded from v1 and the self-contained profile:** multi-user hosted collaboration, automatic multi-machine replication, global knowledge graph canvas, hosted marketplace, messaging bots, native SLX editor, manuscript editors other than the A01 LaTeX workspace, simulation queue, HPC scheduling or automatic restart of external scientific runs (they are observed only), and a PrometheOS-owned general agent loop. Under A05 PrometheOS also never launches, schedules or wakes a coding agent (no scheduled queue pickup, no agent process started by PrometheOS); agents act when the researcher opens a session or asks. Under D13 it builds no literature search or ranking engine, deep-research or question-answering engine, alert engine, chat assistant, notebook or compute workbench, model-based citation reviewer, slide or document generator, generic dashboard builder (saved views over the record are not excluded), memory feature, meeting bot or general search or question-answering product (A07 narrowed this from "bespoke retrieval stack": local hybrid retrieval for linking and proposing records is allowed, R39); it integrates or captures them. A02 permits only bounded, declared literature pipelines: built-in operations with declared inputs, outputs, allowed operations, limits and review points, never arbitrary or model-generated code.

No numerical novelty or market-superiority claim is established by this specification. K-Dense and Orchestra already address notebook and research memory needs; the hypothesis is that continuity across the researcher's independent agent/tool workflow is sufficiently useful.
